\begin{abstract}
We partition distant-stratum false alarms of frozen ESM-2 t30 cosine $k$-NN retrieval by Pfam family membership. At the original $R{=}1000$, $k{=}25$ panel seed 20260410, 41 distant-stratum proteins receive a positive prediction while the true label is negative. Twenty are evaluable under the prespecified annotation rule, and all 20 are CROSS\_FAMILY (zero shared Pfam identifiers with the positive-voting neighbors; per-seed Wilson 95\% interval for the within-family fraction $[0,0.161]$). A subsequently SHA256-locked confirmatory extension repeated the unchanged analysis across the ten already-fixed panel seeds 20260410--20260419. Cross-family false alarms predominated in every seed. The per-seed cross-family fraction had median 1.000, mean 0.994, and range 0.962--1.000; all ten seeds had nonzero evaluable denominators, satisfying the preregistered strong robustness criterion. Across seeds, 102 unique accessions were evaluable: 101 were always cross-family, one was always within-family, and none switched status. The seed, not the recurring accession appearance, is the preregistered robustness unit. Complementary Mapper decomposition provides exploratory geometric context but is not a causal test. The combined evidence disfavors a purely within-family panel-coverage explanation for the cliff in this tested regime; it does not imply that within-family errors are absent or that Pfam exhausts biological relatedness. For deployment, within-family panel expansion should not be relied on as the sole rescue; distant positive predictions still require review, and the panel-only learned projection from Paper 1 is the supported intervention tested here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
Test set: 24{,}885 proteins, 7{,}133 positive (28.7\%). Embeddings: frozen ESM-2 t30 (150M parameters, 640d), $L_2$-normalized. Every panel contains 1{,}000 class-balanced proteins; $k=25$; distant stratum $s_{\max}<0.90$; positive prediction when $\text{votes}\cdot 2 \ge k$. Pfam annotations cover 21{,}615 of 24{,}885 accessions (86.9\%) in \texttt{data/annotations/proteins\_25k\_pfam.json}. The confirmatory extension changes only the reference-panel seed. All ten seeds, the evaluability rule, Pfam partition, aggregation, and decision rule were locked in \texttt{data/prereg/PRE\_REGISTRATION\_CROSS\_FAMILY\_10SEED\_v1.md} before any additional seed was run.

\section{What this disfavors}
A natural explanation for the cliff is a within-family coverage gap: a distant query from family $F$ lacks a close panel homolog from $F$, so adding more members of $F$ should repair retrieval. The original 20/20 result and the preregistered ten-seed panel-composition extension both disfavor that explanation as the dominant mechanism in this dataset and fixed t30/$R{=}1000$/$k{=}25$ regime. Cross-family false alarms outnumber within-family false alarms in every panel seed, and the worst observed seed is still 25/26 (0.962) cross-family among evaluable cases.

The result is not categorical. Within-family errors do occur, as shown by Q5DFZ7 in two seeds. Pfam-ID intersection is also a coarse family definition and cannot exclude shared superfamily, fold, or other biological relationships. The supported claim is therefore cross-family predominance robust to the tested panel-composition perturbation, not that every cliff error is cross-family or that within-family panel expansion can never help an individual case.

\section{Biosecurity deployment consequence}
The ten-seed confirmation strengthens the case against relying on within-family panel expansion as a generic cliff rescue in this tested regime. It does not rule out benefit for a particular query or a different family ontology. A deployer should therefore treat panel expansion as insufficient evidence of safety rather than as a guaranteed failure.

Two actionable consequences follow:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Route every distant-stratum positive prediction to human review, regardless of vote count or $s_{\max}$. Calibration collapse (Paper 3) independently supports this rule.
\item Apply the panel-only learned linear projection (Paper 1) as preprocessing before retrieval. At t30 $R{=}1000$ $k{=}25$, the projection improves distant $F_1$ from 0.120 to 0.177 (47\% relative). This is an empirically tested intervention on the embedding geometry, whereas within-family panel expansion is not supported as a general rescue by the Pfam robustness result.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
(L1) The confirmatory extension tests robustness only to reference-panel composition at fixed ESM-2 t30, $R=1000$, $k=25$, threshold 0.90, dataset, labels, and Pfam ontology; it does not establish invariance across models, $k$, thresholds, or datasets.
(L2) 13\% of test proteins lack Pfam annotations and are excluded from the WITHIN/CROSS denominator under the locked evaluability rule.
(L3) Pfam-ID overlap is a coarse family definition. InterPro superfamily, CATH fold, or structure-aware relations could identify biological relatedness that the Pfam test misses.
(L4) The result at $k=25$ need not hold at $k=1$ or under a different voting rule.
(L5) This is a single-dataset, single-author result and has not yet been independently replicated by a third party.
